1. Two access limitations you should know before reading anything else

The Google Sheet could not be edited from here. docs.google.com is not on this environment's network allowlist, so neither reading nor writing the tracker was possible. Instead of a direct edit, a ready-to-import file is delivered alongside this document: docs/Uenal-SV-Tracker-Status-2026-08-26.csv. In the sheet, use File → Import → Upload and choose "Insert new sheet" — that gives you a dated status sheet next to your original without overwriting it. Every row carries the status, the file-and-line evidence, the owner and the next action.

No build and no browser test could be run. The Linux sandbox for this session failed to start (insufficient disk space on the host), so npx tsc --noEmit, npm run build and a live page render were all unavailable. Every status below therefore comes from static reading of the source code — grep plus reading the files in full. That is reliable for "does this string exist", "is this component mounted", "does this route export metadata" and "is this value in the config". It is not a substitute for looking at the rendered HTML of a production page, and two items (SV-027 duplicate steps and SV-028 unmounted FAQ answers) should be confirmed once in View Source before they are ticked off.

2. Headline: where the project actually stands

Of the 45 tracker lines examined, 17 are finished and need nothing further, 4 more are finished on our side and only wait on information from the client, 9 are partially done (the mechanism exists but does not cover every place it should), 11 are open, 5 (the SEC lines) cannot be statused because their text lives only in the inaccessible sheet, and 1 is superseded by work already delivered.

The single most important correction to the earlier picture: the site is not finished, and the legal pages being finished does not make it compliant. The legal documents themselves — Impressum, Datenschutzerklärung, AGB, Widerrufsbelehrung — are in good shape. What remains is spread across the marketing pages: the standalone use of "zertifiziert" still appears in seven places, one of which is a heading in the footer and therefore on all 600-plus pages; the regional pages render a headline that violates rule 3 because of a lookup bug; and several sections that were supposed to be gone are only hidden with CSS and are still in the HTML that Google reads.

The three non-negotiables are in a good state. No TÜV logo exists anywhere in the project — there is no such image file. None of the three forbidden phrases appear ("TÜV-zertifiziert", "TÜV-geprüft", "TÜV-Sachverständiger": zero occurrences each). The only TÜV mention in the whole codebase is the one line that is supposed to be there, the TRA further-training entry, and it is text only. The qualification block matches the mandated wording character for character, including both certificate numbers, the check digit and both validity dates, and it lives in one central file. Rule 3 — "zertifiziert" never standing alone — is the one that is not yet met, and section 4 lists exactly where.

4. SV-021 — the technology disclosure, delivered now

You asked for this in parallel with the first fixes rather than after them, because the Datenschutzerklärung cannot be finalised without it. Here is the complete inventory, from the dependency manifest and the source, not from assumption.

Framework and rendering

Next.js 15.3.8 with the App Router, React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS for styling. Pages are server-rendered. UI primitives come from Radix (via shadcn/ui) and icons from a local icon package. Animation uses the motion library. Two carousel libraries are present, react-slick and embla. All of these run in the browser or on the server and set no cookies and make no external requests.

What actually leaves the visitor's browser

Only two external destinations, and both are now disclosed in the Datenschutzerklärung:

  • Cloudflare Turnstile (Cloudflare, Inc., 101 Townsend Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA) — the spam protection on the contact forms. The widget script loads from Cloudflare's domain, so Cloudflare receives the visitor's IP address whenever a form page is opened. Disclosed under section 8 of the Datenschutzerklärung on the legal basis of Art. 6(1)(f) DSGVO with the legitimate interest named, and again in section 13 as a possible third-country transfer under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, with standard contractual clauses as the fallback.
  • An external form API at alpa-backend-api.vercel.app, with two endpoints — one for the general contact form and one for the valuation enquiry form. Everything a visitor types into a form is transmitted through this interface. Disclosed in section 8 as processing by a processor under Art. 28 DSGVO.

What is not there — equally important for the privacy policy

  • No analytics of any kind. No Google Analytics, no Plausible, no Matomo, no Vercel Analytics, no Meta or LinkedIn pixel.
  • No Google Fonts and no other font CDN. Fonts are served from the site's own domain — this is the single most common Abmahnung trigger on German sites and it is correctly avoided.
  • No embedded map, no YouTube or Vimeo embed, no social plugin, no chat widget.
  • No cookie banner, because no consent-requiring cookie is set. The Datenschutzerklärung states this explicitly rather than staying silent, which is the defensible position.
  • Reviews and seals are static images and static text — nothing is fetched from Google at runtime.
  • Two review components exist in the codebase but are not reachable from any page. They are dead code and should be deleted; one of them links to the Facility Management business rather than the Sachverständigenbüro (NEW-06).

Four questions that flow directly from this inventory, and that block final sign-off on the Datenschutzerklärung:

  1. Who hosts the site? Almost certainly Vercel given the toolchain, but this must be stated as fact, not inferred. Needed: company, address, and whether a data processing agreement is in place.
  2. Who operates the form API at that address — your own infrastructure or an agency's? A processing agreement under Art. 28 DSGVO is required either way, and the operator has to be nameable.
  3. How long are server logfiles kept? A concrete retention period is required; "as long as necessary" is not sufficient.
  4. Is there a data processing agreement with Cloudflare, and is the Cloudflare entity used the US parent or the European subsidiary? This changes how section 13 must be worded.

10. Method and its limits

Everything above was established by reading the source: the full central config, the legal content file, the build configuration, the routing layer, the sitemap, the page templates and the shared components, plus exhaustive searches for each forbidden and required string across the tree. Where a status says "zero hits", that is a search across the whole source directory, not a spot check.

What that method cannot establish, and what should be confirmed in a browser before these lines are closed: that the duplicated process steps and the missing FAQ answers behave in the delivered HTML exactly as the component code implies; that the four legal pages render without a layout defect on mobile; and that the site compiles cleanly — which matters more than usual here, because the build is configured to ignore type and lint errors, so a broken type would not announce itself.
